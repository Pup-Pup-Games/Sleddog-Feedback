# How to file something useful

There's no code here, so "contributing" means writing a report someone can act
on. This page is about how to do that well — and, just as importantly, what not
to put in a public tracker.

## The one hard rule

> **This repository is public. Everything you write here is readable by anyone,
> forever, including in Google results.**

Never post: your email address, your account details, payment or card
information, invoice numbers, or screenshots showing any of those. Also think
twice about screenshots of your table's private campaign notes — your players
may not expect them to be public.

Anything in that category goes to **support@puppup.games** (or
**privacy@puppup.games** for a data export or deletion request), where we can
handle it properly. You will not get a slower answer for using email; if
anything you'll get a faster one.

Found a security vulnerability? Please use a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/Pup-Pup-Games/Sleddog-Feedback/security/advisories/new),
not a public issue — it gives us a chance to fix it before it's exploitable.

## Filing a bug

**Search first.** A 👍 on an existing report is more useful than a duplicate: it
tells us how many people are hitting it, which is a large part of how things get
prioritised.

Then use the [bug report form](https://github.com/Pup-Pup-Games/Sleddog-Feedback/issues/new?template=bug_report.yml).
The fields that matter most:

- **Steps to reproduce.** The single most valuable thing in the whole report. A
  bug we can reproduce is usually fixed the same week; one we can't may sit for
  months. Rough numbered steps beat a polished paragraph.
- **The app version.** It's at the bottom of every page, and it tells us whether
  you're on a build where this is already fixed.
- **What you expected.** Sometimes the behaviour is intentional and the real bug
  is that we made it confusing — which is still a bug worth knowing about.

One report per bug, please. Three problems in one issue means two of them get
lost when it's closed.

## Requesting a feature

Use the [feature request form](https://github.com/Pup-Pup-Games/Sleddog-Feedback/issues/new?template=feature_request.yml),
and **lead with the problem rather than the solution.**

"I want a duplicate button" tells us one way to solve something. "Every session I
rebuild the same three followers from scratch" tells us what actually hurts — and
often points at a better fix than the one either of us would have guessed.

Say how you handle it today, too. A problem you've papered over with a
spreadsheet is a different priority from one that stops you mid-session.

## What happens next

Issues get triaged — labelled and placed on the right board — usually within a
week. Accepted work appears on the public
[roadmap](https://github.com/orgs/Pup-Pup-Games/projects) and moves through
*Now → Next → Later* as it progresses. When the fix ships, the issue closes and
links the change.

**We won't build everything.** Sled Dog is made by a very small studio, and a
tracker full of five-year-old open requests helps nobody. If we're not going to
do something we'll close it and say why. That's meant as a real answer, not a
brush-off, and you're welcome to argue with it.

## Community content

Authoring arcana, items, NPCs, or expeditions for Sled Dog — or fighting with the
schema validator — is handled in
[SledDog-Community-Content](https://github.com/Pup-Pup-Games/SledDog-Community-Content),
which has its own CONTRIBUTING guide.

## Conduct

Be decent. Assume the person on the other end is trying. Criticism of the
software is always welcome and often deserved; criticism of people isn't.
Reports that are abusive, or that target someone personally, get closed without
discussion.
